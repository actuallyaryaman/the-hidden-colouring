Sources & boundaries.

An implementation and study of an established protocol. The public transcript and the assumptions behind it are both open to inspection.

Primary references

  1. Oded Goldreich, Zero-Knowledge Twenty Years After Its Invention (2004), especially Figure 2 and its explanation.
  2. Goldreich, Zero-Knowledge Proof Systems, graph three-colourability.
  3. Goldreich, Micali & Wigderson, Proofs that yield nothing but their validity, FOCS 1986.
  4. Python documentation: hashlib, secrets, and JSON serialisation.

What this implementation establishes

The verifier checks public graph structure, challenged endpoints, valid openings, full SHA-256 digests, and different endpoint colours. It does not need a witness or a live prover to check an exported record.

A transcript alone cannot prove that a challenge was selected unpredictably. It also cannot establish a graph’s colourability; a simulator can arrange an accepting transcript. Interactive soundness requires challenge timing and cryptographic assumptions.

The salted-hash commitment is an educational instantiation. Hiding needs an appropriate assumption about the construction; collision resistance alone does not imply hiding. Both parties run locally and the teaching graphs are intentionally small and known.
